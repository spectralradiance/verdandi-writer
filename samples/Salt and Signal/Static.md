Forty miles away, in the coastguard station at Harlow Point, Ilse Brandt was listening to the sea through a pair of headphones. Not the sea itself: the sea's noise as it came through the ether, a hiss that rose and fell like breathing, broken by the dots and dashes of ships asking where they were.

She had been told to expect a new voice from Skerrow by the spring. A beacon, the inspector said, that would call its own name every six minutes, day and night, fog or clear. She had written the call sign on a card and pinned it above the set: SKR, dot-dot-dot, dash-dot-dash, dot-dash-dot.

> [!notes]
> Ilse's chapters are short. She is the reader's ear on the mainland.

At night, when the traffic thinned, she sometimes tapped it out on the desk with one finger, practising, as if she could call the island into answering before it had learned to speak.

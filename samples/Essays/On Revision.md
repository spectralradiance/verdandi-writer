# Re-seeing

The word gives the game away. To revise is to see again, and the hardest part of seeing again is forgetting what you meant to say long enough to notice what you actually said.

Most first drafts are letters to ourselves. They are full of private shorthand, of sentences that work only because we remember the feeling that produced them. The reader has no such memory. Revision begins when we agree to become that reader.

# Cutting

Every writer has a sentence they love that the piece does not need. The test is simple and cruel: read the paragraph without it. If nothing is lost, it goes, however much it cost to write.

It helps to keep a file of cuttings. Nothing is destroyed, only moved, and a surprising number of good sentences find a better home later.

# Reordering

Sometimes nothing is wrong with the sentences and everything is wrong with their order. A paragraph that buries its point in the middle can be fixed by moving one line to the top. A chapter that opens slowly can often start at its third page.

# Listening

Read it aloud. Where you stumble, the reader will stumble. Where you run out of breath, the sentence is too long. Where you get bored, so will they, and they will not tell you; they will just stop.
